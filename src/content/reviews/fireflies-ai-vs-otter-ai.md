**TL;DR:** Fireflies.ai and Otter.ai do the same core job — join your calls, transcribe them, summarize them — but they're built for different situations. Fireflies pulls ahead on language support, integration breadth, and per-seat pricing at the entry tier. Otter is simpler and slightly cheaper if you're an English-only team that just wants basic transcription without a lot of extra tooling. Neither is a strict upgrade over the other; it depends on what you actually need beyond "write down what was said."

## A Quick Note on How This Guide Was Put Together

I haven't run both tools side by side on the same real meetings myself — this comparison is built from each company's own product and pricing pages, plus a look through aggregated user reviews for both tools, rather than hands-on parallel testing. If the decision is close for your use case, I'd treat this as a solid shortlist-narrower and still run both free tiers on a real meeting or two before committing to a paid plan.

## The Core Difference in One Line

Fireflies is built to be the more full-featured option — more languages, more integrations, more automation beyond note-taking. Otter is built to be simpler — fewer moving parts, a more focused feature set centered on transcription and basic summaries. Neither approach is wrong; it's a question of whether you want more tool or less tool.

## Transcription Accuracy

Fireflies advertises around 95% transcription accuracy; Otter is generally cited around 85% in third-party comparisons. Take both numbers with some skepticism — they're self-reported or aggregated from comparison sites rather than a controlled independent benchmark run on identical audio. What's more consistent across user reviews is that Fireflies tends to hold up better in group discussions with overlapping speakers, while Otter's accuracy is more reliable in clean, one-on-one audio.

## Language Support

This is one of the clearer differentiators. Fireflies supports 100+ languages, including automatic language detection. Otter's language support is much narrower — English, French, and Spanish. If your team meets in more than one language, or you regularly have non-English calls, this alone may settle the decision in Fireflies' favor.

## Integrations

Fireflies connects to a noticeably wider set of tools — Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams for the meeting side, plus CRMs (Salesforce, HubSpot), and collaboration tools (Slack, Asana, Trello). Otter's integration list is comparatively limited, generally covering the same three video platforms without the same CRM/collaboration depth. If meeting notes need to flow automatically into a CRM or project tool, Fireflies has the edge here.

## Summary Quality

Both generate an automatic post-meeting summary with an overview and action items. In side-by-side comparisons and user reviews, Fireflies' summaries are frequently described as more structured — though a few Fireflies reviewers also flag the opposite problem, summaries that list too many minor action items from a short meeting, so "more structured" isn't always "more useful" without some editing either way.

## Where Otter Still Makes Sense

Despite Fireflies' broader feature set, Otter isn't simply worse — it's a narrower tool, and narrower can be the right choice. A small, English-only team that just wants clean transcripts and basic summaries, without needing CRM integration or multilingual support, may find Otter's simpler interface and slightly lower entry price a better fit. More features you don't use isn't a win.

## Who Should Pick Which

Fireflies is the stronger fit for teams that meet in more than one language, need transcripts to flow into a CRM automatically, or want a broader set of integrations across the tools they already use. Otter is a reasonable choice for smaller, English-only teams that want straightforward transcription without much else attached to it.
