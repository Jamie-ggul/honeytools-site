## Why Listen to Me on This

I started with it for work documentation, moved on to personal schedule and task management, and along the way it became my go-to space for cross-team collaboration: when I need input from another team, I lay out the discussion on a Notion page and have everyone drop their opinions directly on it instead of chasing people across five chat threads. ## What Is Notion, Exactly?

Notion is a flexible page-and-database workspace: simple text notes, structured databases with multiple views (table, board, calendar, list), and everything in between. Instead of forcing you into a rigid template like a typical note app or project tracker, it gives you building blocks and lets you assemble your own structure — which is why it's grown so far beyond "just a note app."

## Who Notion Is Actually For (and Who It's Not)

**A strong fit if you:**

- Work across teams and need one shared place to collect input instead of scattering it across chat and email
- Want to document processes or decisions in a way that's easy to search and reference later
- Like using checklists to manage your own workload and avoid dropping things

**Might frustrate you if you:**

- Need spreadsheet-grade performance for very large datasets — Notion's databases slow down as they grow
- Want zero setup time — Notion rewards the time you invest in structuring it
- Need instant, rock-solid real-time sync for a large, fast-moving team

## Key Features I Actually Use

**Documentation pages.** Nested pages let me keep a high-level overview and drill into details without cluttering the main view — useful for anything from onboarding docs to project retrospectives.

**Databases with multiple views.** The same tasks or notes can show up as a table, a kanban board, or a calendar depending on what's useful in the moment.

**Personal checklists.** A simple checklist database with due dates and status tags. It sounds basic, but it's the single feature that's cut down on missed tasks the most for me.

**Comments for collaboration.** When I need feedback from another team, I write up the context on a page and let people comment directly on the sections that matter to them — keeping the discussion attached to the content instead of buried in chat.

## Pros and Cons After 3 Years

**Pros:** One tool replaces several (docs, tasks, light project tracking) · Flexible structure that adapts to how you work · Great for centralizing team input without losing context

**Cons:** Performance can lag on large, heavily-linked databases · Easy to over-engineer your own setup into a mess · Real learning curve — expect a few weeks before it clicks

## How I Actually Use It

**Cross-team collaboration:** When a decision needs input from another team, I create a page laying out the background and the specific question I need answered, then have people leave opinions as comments directly on it. It keeps everyone's input in one place and makes it much easier to summarize afterward.

**Personal checklist:** A simple database with a checkbox, a due date, and a status tag. Reviewing it each morning has meaningfully cut down on things falling through the cracks.

## FAQ

**Is Notion free?**
Yes — the Free plan covers a lot for individual use. You'll likely want Plus or Business once you're collaborating with a team regularly.

**Is Notion good for team collaboration?**
Yes, especially for centralizing documentation and gathering input across teams. It's less suited to real-time, spreadsheet-heavy collaboration.
