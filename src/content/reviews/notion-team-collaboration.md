**TL;DR:** When I need input from another team, I don't chase people across Slack threads and separate DMs. I lay out the context and the specific question on one Notion page and let everyone drop their opinion directly on it as a comment. It's a small shift that's made cross-team input dramatically easier to collect and summarize.

## Why This Workflow Exists

Over three years of using Notion, this is the piece that's had the biggest actual impact on my day-to-day work — not the databases, not the templates, just this one pattern for getting input from people outside my immediate team. Before I settled into it, getting a decision reviewed by another team meant scattering the same context across chat messages, hoping people saw it, and then manually piecing together everyone's opinions afterward.

## The Structure I Actually Use

**1. One page, one decision.** I don't bundle multiple asks into a single page — each page covers one specific decision or question that needs outside input.

**2. Context first, question second.** The top of the page lays out just enough background for someone with zero context to understand what's being decided and why. Then I state the actual question clearly, usually as its own short section near the top so it's not buried.

**3. Comments, not edits.** I ask people to leave their input as comments directly on the relevant section, rather than editing the page itself. This keeps the original context intact and makes it easy to see who said what, attached to exactly the part of the page it's about.

**4. One place to summarize from.** Because every opinion lives as a comment on the same page, summarizing afterward is just reading through one thread instead of reconstructing a conversation spread across multiple tools.

## Why This Beats Chasing People in Chat

The real win isn't that Notion is a fancier tool than chat — it's that the input stays attached to the context that produced it. Six months later, if someone asks "why did we decide this," the full reasoning and every opinion that shaped it are still sitting on that one page. In chat, that same conversation would already be buried under everything that's happened since.

## Where This Works Best

This pattern is most useful for decisions that need a handful of specific people's input, not broad announcements or ongoing project tracking (Notion's databases are a better fit for that). If you're regularly needing a small number of stakeholders to weigh in on something specific, this is worth trying before defaulting to another chat thread.

## FAQ

**Do people need Notion accounts to comment?**
Depends on your workspace's sharing settings — most setups let you share a page with guest access for people outside your immediate workspace, which is enough for them to view and comment.

**What if people don't check Notion regularly?**
This workflow works best for people who already touch Notion regularly. If your stakeholders live entirely in chat, you may still need to drop a link and a nudge there — Notion becomes the place the actual discussion lives, not necessarily where people get notified.
