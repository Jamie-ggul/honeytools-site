**TL;DR:** Writing professional English as a second language means a specific kind of doubt — not "is this grammatically wrong," but "would a native speaker actually phrase it this way." Grammarly's free version doesn't fully answer that second question, but it reliably catches the first one, and that's been enough to make it a permanent step before I send anything written in English.

## Why Listen to Me on This

English isn't my first language, and a chunk of my work involves writing it anyway — reports, emails, that kind of thing. I've used Grammarly's free version for a while now specifically as a second check before hitting send, not because I don't know the grammar rules, but because it's very easy to miss your own small mistakes in a language you didn't grow up speaking. This isn't a "here's why every non-native writer needs this" pitch — it's just what my actual workflow looks like and where it does and doesn't help.

## The Real Problem It Solves

The hardest mistakes to catch in your own writing are the ones that feel correct to you. A wrong preposition, a missing article, a word that's technically fine but not how a native speaker would actually say it — these don't trigger any internal alarm when you're the one writing them, because they follow patterns that feel natural if you learned English differently. That's the specific gap Grammarly's free checks close: not "do I know grammar," but "did I actually apply it correctly this time, in this sentence, without noticing."

## How I Actually Use It

My workflow is simple and hasn't changed much: I write the report or email normally, then paste the finished text into Grammarly rather than writing directly inside it. I go through the flagged suggestions one by one — most are minor (a preposition, an article, a comma), but every so often it catches something that changes the meaning of the sentence, which is the kind of mistake that actually matters before something goes out to a client or a manager.

I don't accept every suggestion automatically. Some of the "clarity" or phrasing suggestions genuinely aren't better, just different — the grammar and spelling flags are the ones I trust without much second-guessing.

## What It Catches That I'd Miss Myself

- **Article usage (a/an/the)** — one of the most common non-native patterns, and one of the easiest to miss in your own writing since it rarely changes whether a sentence is understandable
- **Preposition choices** — "interested in" vs. "interested on," that category of mistake, where the wrong option is close enough that it doesn't feel obviously wrong
- **Subject-verb agreement in longer sentences** — easy to lose track of once a sentence has a few clauses in it
- **Overly literal phrasing** — sentences that are grammatically fine but translate a little too directly from how I'd phrase the same idea in my first language

## What It Doesn't Fix

Grammarly's free tier is a grammar and spelling check, not a native-fluency filter. It won't tell you that a sentence is grammatically correct but sounds stiff, overly formal, or just not how someone would actually phrase it in casual professional English — that kind of feedback needs a native speaker, or increasingly, an AI tool asked directly to comment on how natural something sounds. I've started using AI for that second layer on longer documents, while free Grammarly stays the default first pass for anything shorter.

## Is Free Enough for This?

For catching the mistakes above, yes — I've used the free tier this way for a while and haven't felt limited by it. ## FAQ

**Is Grammarly actually useful if I already know English grammar rules well?**
In my experience, yes — knowing the rules and reliably applying them under time pressure, in your second language, aren't quite the same thing. It's less about not knowing and more about a second set of eyes.

**Should I write directly in Grammarly, or write first and paste in after?**
I paste in after writing. Writing first lets me focus on the actual content without interruption, then I do a dedicated pass for correctness afterward. Either works — this is just what fits my workflow better.

## Final Thoughts

If your English writing already gets the job done but you're never quite sure whether a sentence reads the way you meant it to, free Grammarly is a low-effort second check that's earned a permanent spot in my process. It won't make your writing sound more native on its own, but it reliably catches the kind of small, easy-to-miss mistakes that are hardest to see in your own writing.
