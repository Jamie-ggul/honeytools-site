**TL;DR:** I've used Grammarly for a while now, always the same way — write somewhere else, then paste the finished text in to check it. I only recently found out there's a browser extension that checks your writing inline, wherever you're typing, without the copy-paste step at all. It's not a different product, just a different way of getting the same checks — and whether it's worth turning on depends entirely on where you actually do most of your writing.

## Why Listen to Me on This

This isn't a "here's my extension review after using it for months" post — it's the opposite, and I think that's actually useful context. I've used Grammarly's free version for proofreading finished writing for a while, but always by pasting text into the Grammarly editor after I'd finished writing somewhere else. I genuinely didn't realize there was a browser extension that does the same checking live, inline, in Gmail, Google Docs, or wherever else you're typing — until recently. So this is less a seasoned take and more an honest look at what I've been missing by sticking to copy-paste, and whether it's actually worth changing.

## What the Extension Actually Adds Over Copy-Paste

The checking itself isn't different — it's still the same grammar, spelling, and punctuation engine. What changes is when you see the suggestions. With copy-paste, you write everything first, then find out about mistakes in a separate pass at the end. With the extension installed, it flags issues as you type, directly inside Gmail, Google Docs, Slack, LinkedIn, and most other text fields on the web — no separate step, no remembering to paste your draft in before sending.

For anything short — an email, a Slack message, a form field — that difference matters more than it sounds like it should. Pasting a two-line email into a separate tab just to check it is exactly the kind of small friction that makes people skip the check entirely.

## Where You'll Actually See It

Once installed, it shows up as a small floating icon in text fields across the browser, plus underlines on flagged words or phrases as you type — click either to see the suggestion and accept or dismiss it. It works across a long list of sites and apps (Gmail, Docs, LinkedIn, Zendesk, Jira, and most standard text boxes), not just a hand-picked few.

## Setup — What to Expect

It's a standard browser extension install (available for Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge) — add it, sign in with the same free account you'd already use on grammarly.com, and it starts working in supported text fields without further setup. There's a settings option to turn it off for specific sites if you don't want it active everywhere, which matters if you'd rather not have it running somewhere like a password field or a site with sensitive text.

## When It's Actually Worth Turning On

Based on what it actually changes, the extension makes the most sense if a meaningful chunk of your writing happens live, in short bursts — emails, chat messages, comments, forms. That's exactly the writing that's easy to skip checking when the only option is a separate copy-paste step.

If most of your writing is long-form — reports, articles, anything you draft somewhere else and finish before it needs checking — the paste-in workflow I've been using loses less by comparison, since you were never going to check it mid-sentence anyway.

## FAQ

**Does the extension cost anything extra beyond the free plan?**
No — it uses the same free (or paid) account you already have. It's a different way of accessing the same checks, not a separate product tier.

**Can I turn it off for specific websites?**
Yes, there's a per-site toggle in the extension's settings if you don't want it running everywhere.

## Final Thoughts

Realistically, the fact that I went this long without knowing the extension existed says something on its own — a paste-in workflow works fine if most of your writing is longer documents you finish before checking. But for anything typed live and sent quickly, this is the more obvious way to use Grammarly, and it's a genuinely easy switch to make once you know it's there.
