**Update, November 19, 2026:** The IPO has priced. On November 18, Anthropic [announced](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering) a price of $83.00 per share, above the $70 to $78 range from the roadshow, and a larger offering of 980 million shares. ANT started trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19. We've replaced the roadshow terms with the final terms, added what's been reported about the first day of trading, and kept the October investor-day background further down. Our November 15 update covered the roadshow launch.

**TL;DR:** Anthropic [priced](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering) its initial public offering at **$83.00 per share** on November 18, 2026. The offering is 980,000,000 Class A shares (956,351,354 sold by Anthropic, 23,648,646 by existing stockholders), up from 825 million at the roadshow, and underwriters have a 30-day option on up to 147,000,000 more. Shares were expected to begin trading on November 19 on Nasdaq under the ticker **ANT**, with the deal expected to close November 20. By our arithmetic, that's about $81.3 billion in gross proceeds before the extra shares. Press reports say ANT's first trade was $111.50, about 34% above the IPO price, before the stock gave back part of that gain during the day. None of this is investment advice.

## What Anthropic Has Confirmed (November 18 Pricing Announcement)

These come from Anthropic's [pricing announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering), published November 18, 2026 and checked November 19, 2026:

| Item | Confirmed detail | Change from the Nov 10 roadshow |
|---|---|---|
| Price per share | $83.00 | Above the $70 to $78 range |
| Shares offered | 980,000,000 Class A common shares | Up from 825,000,000 |
| Sold by Anthropic | 956,351,354 shares | Up from 801,351,354 |

| Extra shares for underwriters | 30-day option on up to 147,000,000 additional shares | Up from an expected 123,750,000 |
| Exchange and ticker | Nasdaq Global Select Market, symbol "ANT" | Unchanged |
| Expected first trading day | November 19, 2026 | Newly announced |
| Expected closing | November 20, 2026, subject to customary closing conditions | Newly announced |
| SEC status | Registration statement declared effective on November 18, 2026 | Was "not yet effective" |
| Where to get the prospectus | Goldman Sachs & Co. LLC and Morgan Stanley & Co. LLC | Unchanged |

Some quick arithmetic (ours, not Anthropic's): 980 million shares at $83 is about $81.3 billion in gross proceeds, before underwriting fees and before the 147 million optional shares. If the underwriters buy all of those too, the total would be about $93.5 billion. Not all of it goes to Anthropic, since part of the offering is sold by existing stockholders. Gross proceeds are the size of the offering, not the company's valuation.

## What Has Been Reported About Pricing and Day One

These are press reports. Anthropic hasn't confirmed them in its own announcements:

- **First trade:** Stock Titan's live coverage reports ANT's first trade at $111.50 at 11:48 a.m. ET on November 19, out of Nasdaq's opening auction, about 34% above the $83 IPO price. NBC News, as relayed by other outlets, put it at about 11:50 a.m.
- **Intraday range:** Stock Titan reports a high of $114.99 shortly after the open and a low of $92.72 around 1:18 p.m. ET, with the stock near $96 in mid-afternoon. We didn't have a confirmed closing price when we published this update.
- **Valuation:** CNBC (as relayed by other outlets) put Anthropic's market value at about $2 trillion at the first-trade price. That figure depends on the share count used and moves with the stock, so treat it as a rough snapshot.
- **Size record:** CNBC and others describe it as the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX's offering earlier this year. We haven't checked the comparison against the SpaceX filings.
- **Side sale to tech companies:** Coverage of the filings (Stock Titan) describes a separate $26 billion sale of non-voting Class N shares at $83 to several large technology companies, set to close after the IPO. Anthropic's pricing announcement doesn't mention it, so treat the details as reported until you check the final prospectus.
- **Demand:** Before pricing, Bloomberg reported the order book was multiple times oversubscribed, and the Financial Times (as relayed by Fortune) reported bankers were considering pricing above the range, which is what happened.

## Background: The October 14 Investor Day

Prospective investors met CEO Dario Amodei and CFO Krishna Rao in San Francisco, per Reuters. Here is what attendees and reporters said, with the source attached to each:

| Item | What was reported | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 2026 revenue | About $16.8 billion | FT first; Reuters (two attendees); Bloomberg (people familiar) |
| Growth | Up about 45% from Q2 (about $11.6 billion); Q1 was about $4.7 billion | Reuters, unnamed sources |
| Profitability | Profitable on an adjusted basis in Q3; no profit figure given | Attendees, per Reuters |
| Cash flow | Cash-flow positive in the quarter | FT only, per The Next Web |
| Year-end run rate | Analysts who met executives estimate up to about $110 billion annualized | Reuters, analyst estimate |
| 2025 revenue | Nearly $4.6 billion, up roughly twelvefold, with an operating loss above $8 billion that excluded some liability write-downs | Reuters |

A few cautions on these numbers. The Q3 figure is quarterly revenue, not an annualized run rate. "Adjusted" profit hasn't been defined in any of the reports, so we don't know which costs it leaves out. Reuters also notes that Anthropic has committed at least $518 billion over a decade to cloud services, computing, and infrastructure, which is the other side of the growth story. And the $110 billion figure is an outside estimate, not company guidance.

Attendees also told Reuters that Amodei reiterated the company's focus on AI safety. Reuters notes that Anthropic's prospectus, which it reviewed last month, warns of "catastrophic or existential risks" from AI, so the safety messaging is part of the investor pitch.

## How the Timeline Moved

- **June 1, 2026:** Anthropic confidentially submitted a draft S-1 to the SEC.
- **September–October:** Reports floated an October listing, then a November one after the U.S. midterm elections. In early October, Bloomberg reported formal marketing could start the week of November 9, at a valuation some investors put at $1.8 trillion to $2 trillion.
- **October 26:** Anthropic publicly filed its S-1.
- **November 10:** Roadshow launched with a $70 to $78 range, which reporting put at roughly $1.4 trillion.
- **November 18:** Registration declared effective; IPO priced at $83 with 980 million shares (confirmed).
- **November 19:** ANT began trading on Nasdaq (expected per Anthropic; first trades reported by the press).
- **November 20:** Expected closing date, per Anthropic.

Anthropic's public statements on the IPO are its S-1 filing announcement (October 26), the roadshow announcement (November 10), and the pricing announcement (November 18). ## What We Still Don't Know

- **Whether the extra 147 million underwriter shares get bought:** The option runs for 30 days after pricing.
- **Where ANT settles:** First-day prices for very large IPOs often swing widely, and one day of trading says little about the long run.
- **The final details of the Class N side sale:** Its terms are in the filings, not in Anthropic's announcement, and it was reported as not yet closed.
- **The first earnings report as a public company:** Until then, the Q3 numbers above remain reported figures, not audited quarterly results in a public filing.

## Why This Matters Beyond Wall Street

For people who use or build on Claude, an IPO is mostly a signal about scale and funding rather than about the product day to day. Frontier AI companies are spending heavily on computing infrastructure, and going public is one way to raise capital for that. It also puts AI developers under the kind of public-company scrutiny, with regular financial reporting and shareholder pressure, that can change how they price and release models. ## What This Means for Ordinary Investors

Now that ANT is listed, anyone with a brokerage account that offers Nasdaq stocks can buy or sell shares on the open market, at the market price rather than the $83 IPO price. IPO allocations at the offering price went mostly to institutional investors. Large first-day swings are common with big offerings, as ANT's own first day showed. This is general background, not advice about whether to buy anything, and we're not financial advisors.
